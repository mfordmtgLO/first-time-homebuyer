# Lender-Realtor Single-Property Page: Neutral Compliance Fact Pattern

## Purpose and limitation

This is a neutral issue-spotting checklist for corporate compliance or qualified counsel. It does not conclude that a planned arrangement is lawful or unlawful and does not replace legal advice. The proposed workflow involves lender-branded, potentially co-branded single-property pages; MLS-sourced listing information; Roadmap and mortgage-chat lead capture; page assignment to a loan officer and agent; lender/agent email drafts; and potential agent visibility or follow-up.

## Official Section 8 framing

The CFPB states that RESPA Section 8(a) prohibits giving or accepting a fee, kickback, or other thing of value pursuant to an agreement or understanding for referrals of business incident to a settlement service involving a federally related mortgage loan. Regulation X defines “thing of value” broadly and says it does not require a transfer of money. An agreement or understanding may be written, oral, or shown through a practice, pattern, or course of conduct. [1] [2]

The CFPB also states that normal promotional or educational activities must not be conditioned on referrals and must not defray expenses otherwise incurred by a person in a position to refer settlement-service business. Whether either condition is met is fact-specific. [1] [2]

> “The source of the payment does not determine whether or not a service is compensable.” — 12 CFR § 1024.14(c) [2]

## Why separate software subscriptions do not answer the question

The fact that a Realtor pays MLS subscriptions while a loan officer or lender pays a software subscription is relevant background, but it does not by itself resolve the character of the arrangement. Compliance should evaluate the aggregate value exchanged and the operating pattern: agent-branded exposure, listing photos/remarks, site creation, host costs, distribution, paid advertising, consumer lead capture, lead routing, preferred follow-up, referral expectations, exclusivity, and the frequency with which a particular agent receives the benefit.

## Fact-pattern questions for corporate compliance or counsel

| Review area | Questions to resolve before activation |
|---|---|
| **Purpose and eligibility** | Is the property-page tool available on uniform terms to an appropriate class of agents, or is it limited to actual/potential referral sources? Is access independent of past, current, or expected referrals? |
| **Costs and value** | Who pays for hosting, creative work, MLS/IDX feed access, lead forms, AI drafting, paid distribution, agent-branded placement, and data enrichment? Would the agent otherwise purchase any of those services? |
| **Lead routing** | Does the page route consumer leads to the loan officer, the agent, both, or either based on consumer choice? Is any routing, access, prioritization, or reporting conditioned on referrals, listing volume, or funded-loan volume? |
| **Marketing claims** | Does each page clearly identify the loan officer, lending company, listing brokerage/agent, required MLS attribution, and source/freshness? Are finance/program claims approved and non-guaranteed? |
| **Advertising** | Is lender-paid advertising featuring a particular agent/listing allowed under company policy and data license? Is agent participation tied to referral behavior or treated as a separately valued marketing service? |
| **Documentation** | Is there a written compliance approval, applicable MLS/vendor license, required broker approval, billing record, page/audit trail, and a process to retire pages when listing status or permissions change? |
| **Governance** | Who approves the partner, template, source feed, page assignment, lead handling, page renewal, and exception requests? How are conflicts, complaints, and audits handled? |

## Recommended product safeguards pending approval

1. Keep property pages and source feeds in a **Compliance pending** state until written approval is recorded.
2. Do not tie page creation, agent branding, lead routing, reporting, paid promotion, or enhancements to referral/funded-loan volume.
3. Maintain a durable audit trail for page creation, pair assignment, public/retired state, data refresh, share events, captured leads, and dashboard actions.
4. Preserve consumer choice and clear agent/loan-officer identity; do not represent an agent as required for financing or a lender as required for a property.
5. Require current MLS/vendor display rights and immediately retire or restrict pages when a source reports a withdrawal, status limitation, or lost permission.
